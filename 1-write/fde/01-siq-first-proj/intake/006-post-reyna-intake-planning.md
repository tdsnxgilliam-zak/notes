## What surfaced

- **Define the operating model more clearly.** The discussion called for a stronger scope and a model that moves success criteria forward without eroding what already exists. Start with the IT outcomes the work should drive; describe how the work reaches an integrated, usable end state.
- **Protect the core and make capacity tradeoffs explicit.** Document what must be preserved and protected, how proposed work avoids damaging it, and what might need to be pulled back to create capacity. The systems, protections, decision maker and actual tradeoffs still need confirmation.
- **Connect the experience and reuse existing solutions.** The notes call for avoiding point solutions and integrating existing solutions wherever possible. They raise the question of what belongs in the core versus what can stand alone, while keeping access, partner and selling experiences seamless. They also point toward end-user-direct and autonomous distribution; the precise problem and scope remain to be defined.
- **Make the platform decision on evidence.** The discussion suggests that technical answers may exist through APIs and data connections, leaving the value of the cost structure as a central question. The relevant comparison, full costs, decision owner and person responsible for informing that decision are not established in the notes. Treat the cost concern as a question to investigate, not a conclusion.
- **Define success and reporting.** The discussion asks how success will be measured and reported. No metric, baseline, target or reporting cadence is recorded. “Environment will be the source of truth” is an incomplete note; clarify which environment and what information it governs.

These themes suggest work on both the pod's operating model and a potential business problem. They do not yet identify a single confirmed capstone or the specific business decision it will improve.

## Proposed next steps

1. **Map the current state with the people doing the work.** Identify the affected users, process, existing solutions and handoffs. Document the core to protect and the experience that must remain seamless.
2. **Develop a bounded candidate.** Describe the problem, intended users, scope, integration opportunities, dependencies and what the pod could demonstrate by January. Identify what would be deferred or require capacity from another team.
3. **Understand the platform timeline.** Ask the platform decision owner when a decision is expected, what milestones or dependencies could affect that timing, and when the pod can expect enough direction to plan its work.
4. **Agree measurement and cadence.** Define a baseline, candidate outcome and guardrail measures, sources, owner and reporting rhythm. Confirm the template's suggested sponsor touchpoints and the day-to-day contact.

The intake template asks for a summary within 48 hours, a short recap to the president, and a summary for Session 2; it also says capstone problems are locked in Session 3. Check the current program timing and whether those steps have already occurred before treating them as outstanding commitments.
